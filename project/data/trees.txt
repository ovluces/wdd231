
1. Yellow Poui / Golden Trumpet Tree

• Scientific Name: Handroanthus chrysanthus (formerly Tabebuia chrysantha)
• Spanish Name: Araguaney (Venezuela's National Tree)
• Why it works: Highly prized for its spectacular bright yellow blooms. It responds well to severe pruning and training, making it a symbolic and beautiful tropical bonsai choice.

2. Pink Poui / Pink Trumpet Tree

• Scientific Name: Tabebuia rosea
• Spanish Name: Apamate
• Why it works: Similar to the Araguaney, it features beautiful pink or purple flowers. It develops a sturdy trunk relatively quickly and responds great to root reduction.

3. Lignum Vitae / Holywood

• Scientific Name: Guaiacum officinale
• Spanish Name: Guayacán
• Why it works: Famous for having some of the densest, hardest wood in the world. It is highly valued in bonsai for its beautiful blue flowers, naturally small leaves, and striking rough bark texture.

4. West Indian Mahogany

• Scientific Name: Swietenia mahagoni / Swietenia macrophylla
• Spanish Name: Caoba
• Why it works: Mahogany trees adapt incredibly well to pot culture. Defoliation techniques easily reduce their compound leaf size, and they naturally form powerful, thick trunks.

5. Saman / Rain Tree

• Scientific Name: Samanea saman
• Spanish Name: Samán
• Why it works: Known for its iconic wide umbrella canopy in the Venezuelan llanos. In bonsai form, its delicate mimosa-like leaves close at night or during rainfall, creating a highly dynamic miniature tree.

6. West Indian Elm / Bay Cedar

• Scientific Name: Guazuma ulmifolia
• Spanish Name: Guásimo
• Why it works: This native tree has excellent back-budding capabilities, meaning it pushes new growth easily from old wood after aggressive pruning. Its serrated leaves scale down nicely.

7. Divi-divi

• Scientific Name: Libidibia coriaria (formerly Caesalpinia coriaria)
• Spanish Name: Dividive
• Why it works: Famous for its wind-swept shapes in coastal regions like Falcón. Its naturally twisted trunks and tiny leaflets make it an exceptional candidate for the Fukinagashi (windswept) bonsai style.

8. Brazilian Raintree

• Scientific Name: Chloroleucon tortum
• Spanish Name: Espinito / Olivo Negro (adapted/cultivated)
• Why it works: Native to South America and highly popular in Venezuelan bonsai circles. It features a unique, naturally contorted, fluted trunk and compound leaves that fold up at night.

9. Wild Calabash / Black Calabash

• Scientific Name: Amphitecna latifolia / Crescentia cujete
• Spanish Name: Totumito / Taparo
• Why it works: The Totumito variety is popular due to its compact growth, rugged bark, and high tolerance to wiring and bending.

10. Jaboticaba / Brazilian Grape Tree

• Scientific Name: Plinia cauliflora
• Spanish Name: Jabuticaba / Guaperó
• Why it works: A tropical favorite that thrives in Venezuela. It is famous for cauliflory (producing flowers and purple edible fruits directly on the mature trunk and branches), offering a spectacular look as a miniature.

11. Calliandra / Powderpuff

• Scientific Name: Calliandra houstoniana / Calliandra purpurea
• Spanish Name: Cují de Jardín / Clavellina
• Why it works: Part of the legume family, it possesses very fine foliage and features beautiful, bright red or pink "powderpuff" blossoms that stand out sharply on small-scale designs.

12. Common Guava

• Scientific Name: Psidium guajava
• Spanish Name: Guayabo / Guayaba
• Why it works: Guava trees are excellent for bonsai because of their beautiful peeling bark, exposing a smooth, multicolored trunk beneath. They reduce leaf size efficiently and produce fragrant flowers and miniature fruits.

13. Flamboyant / Royal Poinciana

• Scientific Name: Delonix regia
• Spanish Name: Flamboyán / Acacia
• Why it works: Although originally from Madagascar, it is deeply embedded in the Venezuelan landscape. It requires patience to get the compound leaves to reduce, but rewards the grower with stunning fiery-red umbrella canopies.

14. Strangler Fig / Wild Fig

• Scientific Name: Ficus prinoides / Ficus nymphaeifolia
• Spanish Name: Matapalo / Ficus Nativo
• Why it works: Native Venezuelan Ficus species are incredibly resilient. They develop massive surface roots (nebari) and aggressive aerial roots that can wrap around stones, making them perfect for the "root-over-rock" bonsai style.

15. Pitch Apple / Scotch Attorney

• Scientific Name: Clusia rosea / Clusia minor
• Spanish Name: Copey
• Why it works: These thick, glossy-leaved trees are incredibly hardy. The smaller-leafed variety (Clusia minor) is preferred for bonsai, developing thick, interesting trunks and tolerance to low-water conditions.
